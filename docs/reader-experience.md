# Reader experience

vox is designed to meet a reader inside an existing public conversation. The reader should not need to open a research dashboard or translate a heated thread into search terms before getting useful context.

## Encounter

A reader reaches a public post that assumes history they do not have. The post may be factual, argumentative, humorous, emotional, or deliberately vague. vox can indicate that a related Story exists or let the reader ask for one.

The post remains the starting point. vox does not replace the original speaker or hide the surrounding conversation.

## Compact Story

The first view answers the urgent questions:

- What happened?
- What is the main disagreement?
- Which perspectives matter?
- What changed recently?

The compact Story favors context over controls. It gives the reader enough to understand the shape of the situation without turning the feed into a report.

## Complete Story

A reader who wants more can open the complete Story. It can include:

- neutral context;
- a timeline;
- the main perspectives and their reasons;
- the evidence supporting factual statements;
- a bounded account of retrieved public reaction;
- differences between communities or regions when the material supports them;
- related Stories; and
- meaningful updates and corrections.

vox omits sections that the available material cannot support.

## Follow and revisit

Public situations change after the first explanation appears. A reader can follow a Story and return when a meaningful development changes the context, timeline, perspectives, or evidence.

An update does not need to manufacture novelty. If new material does not change the Story, the supported explanation remains in place.

## Contribute without starting another comment section

A reader may have relevant context or a missing perspective. vox can accept that input as a non-public Contribution for later Story analysis.

A Contribution can help explain a perspective or reason. It cannot establish a factual claim by itself. Factual context still needs independent support. The full submission and the reader's account do not become a public comment attached to the Story.

## Report a problem

A reader can identify a problem with a specific part of a Story. Reports can concern weak support, missing context, privacy, safety, impersonation, or abuse.

A report starts review. It does not let one reader silently rewrite the Story or remove an ordinary disagreement.

## Illustrative example

A creator posts that a platform changed how it pays artists. Reactions split quickly. Some people call the change a pay cut. Others say the new terms help smaller accounts. Screenshots circulate without dates.

A vox Story could separate the platform's published terms from creator reactions, establish when the change took effect, explain the main interpretations, and show only the reaction patterns found in the retrieved material. If the platform later changes the terms, the Story can add that development without pretending the earlier discussion never happened.

The example is fictional. It demonstrates the reader experience, not product availability or a real event.
